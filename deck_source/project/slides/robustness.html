Saving is positive on every simulated day; a timer just under the break length is the worst choice.

Timer study is the strongest engineering result. With T1 = 10 min a machine sleeps 10 minutes into a 15-minute tea break and the operator returns within 5 minutes: 1.75 nuisance events per machine-shift. T1 = 3 min gives more saving (8.6 %) and fewer nuisance events (0.30). T1 = 1 min raises false standbys when the operator is briefly hidden. Better still: send a planned pause at the start of scheduled breaks. Sensitivity (report, S6): the idle-to-processing power ratio matters most, so it must be measured first.
