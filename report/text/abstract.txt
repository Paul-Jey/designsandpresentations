Machines in small and medium machining shops keep drawing power long after they stop cutting. Pumps, fans, lights and compressors run through breaks, material waits and sometimes whole nights. This project develops an edge artificial intelligence layer that uses existing surveillance cameras to detect this waste and act on it, while keeping all video on the device.

In the first phase of the work, an edge node was built on an NVIDIA Jetson Nano running DeepStream with a TensorRT-optimised ResNet10 person detector. The node turns camera frames into a filtered person count and publishes it to a live dashboard. Following feedback from industry experts, the decision layer was then extended from an office room to a manufacturing shop. Operator presence in a region of interest at each machine is combined with machine activity from a current transformer clamp, and a four-state machine moves a machine to standby only when it is both idle and unattended, with a further deep standby for machines that are safe to power down. The same signals control zone lighting, ventilation fans, the conveyor and the compressor.

The electrical effect was evaluated with a stochastic simulation of a reference shop with ten CNC machining centres working two shifts, using the Tamil Nadu HT-I tariff of Rs 7.50 per kWh and Rs 608 per kVA per month.
