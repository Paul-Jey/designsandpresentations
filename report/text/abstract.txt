Machines in small machining shops keep drawing power long after they stop cutting, and the pumps, fans, lights and compressors around them run through breaks and material waits. This project develops an edge artificial intelligence layer that uses existing surveillance cameras to detect this waste and act on it, while keeping all video on the device. An edge node built on an NVIDIA Jetson Nano with DeepStream and a TensorRT-optimised ResNet10 detector converts camera frames into a filtered person count. Operator presence at each machine is combined with machine activity from a current transformer clamp, and a four-state machine moves a machine to standby only when it is both idle and unattended. The same signals control lighting, ventilation fans, the conveyor and the compressor. A stochastic simulation of a ten-machine, two-shift shop under the Tamil Nadu HT-I tariff gave a mean energy saving of {mean_pct} % over 1,000 days, equal to {kwh_yr} kWh, Rs {rs_lakh} lakh and {co2} tonnes of CO2 per year, with a payback of about {pb_full} months. Maximum demand was almost unchanged, so the benefit is a reduction in energy rather than in peak demand.
